"Be confident" is the worst dating advice because it skips a step: you are supposed to *become* confident before you can *be* confident. That gap is where most people get stuck.

Confidence is a habit stack, not a switch. Here are nine habits that compound for dating, especially if mainstream apps have made you feel invisible.

## 1. Stop auditioning in your photos

Mainstream dating-photo advice was written for a narrow body type. Ignore it. Use:

- **One full-body photo** in a setting that flatters the room you are in. Good light beats good angles.
- **One photo of you laughing.** Not posed. Asked someone to make you laugh and caught it.
- **One photo of you doing something.** Cooking, hiking, painting, holding a dog.

Photos should let someone *recognise you* on a coffee date, not look like a different person.

## 2. Lead the description with what you like, not what you are

"Plus-size, sapiosexual, INFJ" is a label list. "I make a sourdough loaf every Sunday and I read in the bath" is a person.

## 3. Use platforms whose users opted in

A body-positive dating platform attracts people who actively *want* what you are. Mainstream apps force you to reach a tiny subset of users through a sea of indifferent ones. The math matters.

## 4. Filter early, kindly

A simple line in your bio — "Looking for someone who is excited about dating someone like me" — filters out 80% of bad matches before message one. It also reads as warm, not defensive.

## 5. The 24-hour rule

If a message makes you feel small, do not reply for 24 hours. Half the time you stop wanting to. The other half, you write a calmer response than the wounded one.

## 6. Date the right kind of nervous

Pre-date butterflies are normal. Pre-date dread is data. Listen to the dread.

## 7. Outfit you already love

Not "an outfit that hides things." An outfit you already feel good in. Repeat it across first dates if you want. They will not notice.

## 8. The two-question test

After a date, ask yourself two questions:

- Did I feel like myself?
- Did I feel safe?

Anything below "yes, mostly" on both is not a maybe. It is a no.

## 9. The audience math

If five people on a body-positive platform matched you well in a month, that is more useful than 500 swipe-rights on a general app that go nowhere. Audience quality > audience size.
